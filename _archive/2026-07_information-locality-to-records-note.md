# Research Note: From Information Locality to Information Realism — the Origin of the Records Postulate

**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Status:** provenance + motivational argument, notebook grade — not incorporated into any paper; candidate passages drafted and held here
**Series:** origin records, dated at capture
**Arose from:** a conversation about how the framework resolves Bell's dilemma (realism vs. locality), which surfaced the argument chain that originally led to the records postulate.

---

## The argument chain

The records postulate — information exists only as physical records — did not
originate as a formal convenience. Its origin is an asymmetry that the
Copenhagen reading of entanglement leaves unexplained.

1. **Grant Copenhagen everything it asks.** Alice measures her half of an
   entangled pair; on the collapse reading, Bob's distant particle changes
   instantly.
2. **One structural fact survives untouched: Bob learns nothing.** No record
   anywhere near Bob is altered until a signal from Alice arrives. The
   correlation becomes information in anyone's hands only when the notes are
   compared, at light speed or slower. The no-signaling theorem guarantees
   this — but within Copenhagen it stands as a coincidence: the ontology is
   permitted to act nonlocally while the information about it remains
   strictly causal, and nothing in the interpretation says why information
   should be better behaved than the world it describes.
3. **So: information is local.** (Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics
   reaches neighboring territory, by a different route — from the relativity
   of quantum states toward an informational reading. Here the direction of
   travel is the reverse.)
4. **The forcing question: how can information be local and not real?
   Local *where*?** Locality is a property of things at places. If
   information is the quantity that respects locality, and locality is a
   physical constraint, then information is a physical thing, and it must be
   local *in* something.
5. **Answer: records.** Information is stored in finite physical
   arrangements of degrees of freedom. Information is local because records
   are; nothing else about quantum correlations is required to be.

## Why the chain matters to the framework

The postulate motivated by this chain is the same postulate that later
dissolves Bell. Once every fact must be stored in some record, facts that no
record stores — the outcomes of unperformed measurements — are not facts at
all, and Bell-type derivations lose the joint distribution they require
(Fine's theorem; CHSH paper, Prop. 2). The loop closes: the argument runs
Copenhagen → information locality → information realism → records, and the
theory returns records → no counterfactual definiteness → Bell dissolved with
realism and locality both intact. The concession the Bell analysis makes was
arrived at upstream of Bell-type derivations, not reverse-engineered to evade
them — which answers, in advance, the natural referee objection that
counterfactual definiteness was assumed away to dodge the theorem.

## Dating

The chain predates the formal framework: it belongs to the line of thinking
that led from the interpretation-of-QM reading (Copenhagen, then Rovelli)
toward the 2024 "Quantum Eternalism" formulation, in which observation is
already treated as change in an observer's information state (see
`2024-04_quantum-eternalism-summary.md`, when added). Captured in explicit
five-step form on 2026-07-23.

## Open questions before any promotion

- Whether the paper is the right home at all, versus letting the postulates
  stand without narrative motivation (the papers' current discipline) and
  keeping origin material to the site or the archive.
- Whether step 4 ("local *where*?") should be tightened: "local" in the
  no-signaling sense is a statement about what records can change where,
  which comes close to presupposing the record vocabulary the chain is meant
  to motivate. A version that flags this — motivation, not derivation —
  would be the more defensible form.
- The Rovelli comparison deserves a citation-level check against Rovelli
  1996 §II (information as the primitive) before appearing in print: the
  direction-of-travel claim should be his stated order of argument, not a
  paraphrase from memory.
